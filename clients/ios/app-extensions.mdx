<Badge color="purple">Delegated trust</Badge>

An iOS extension has its own component key, session family, feature grant, and
quota attribution. Its containing app provisions that key after the root is
trusted. The root does not share its DPoP key or refresh chain with the
extension.

<Warning>
  Delegated trust proves that the directly trusted root authorized a bounded
  extension key. It does not prove that the extension independently completed
  App Attest. Apple does not support App Attest key generation from these
  extension contexts.
</Warning>

## Follow the component path

1. [Understand app-extension components](/build/app-extensions/overview).
2. configure [Keychain access groups](/build/app-extensions/keychain-access-groups).
3. complete [containing-app provisioning](/build/app-extensions/containing-app-provisioning).
4. choose the [WidgetKit](/build/app-extensions/widgetkit) or
   [share extension](/build/app-extensions/share-extension) execution guide.
5. review the bounded
   [direct-attestation step-up](/build/app-extensions/direct-attestation-step-up)
   contract without assuming it is available in every extension runtime.

## Lifecycle requirements

- provision only a configured Component Definition;
- seal the requested feature set to the definition and root grant;
- keep the extension's private group and key independent;
- handle first launch before provisioning with
  `containing_app_setup_required`;
- revoke one component without silently revoking its siblings; and
- revoke or retire delegated descendants when root trust policy requires it.

## Verify

Inspect the extension request and confirm its component ID differs from the
root, its trust provenance is delegated, its DPoP key belongs to the extension,
and its feature and quota attribution are component-scoped. Signing and
embedding an extension without observing an executed delegated request proves
provisioning only.
